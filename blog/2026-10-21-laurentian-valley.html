Closing Costs and Financial Steps for Buying a Home.

Essential financial checkpoints, Ontario tax rules, and local rural purchasing considerations across Laurentian Valley and Renfrew County.

Published by Prakash Solanki, REALTOR® October 21, 2026

Nestled along the borders of Pembroke and Petawawa, the township of Laurentian Valley offers buyers the perfect blend of picturesque rural acreage, quiet residential subdivisions, and fast access to major regional hubs such as Canadian Forces Base (CFB) Petawawa and the Pembroke Regional Hospital. Whether you are relocating for a military posting, upgrading to a larger country property, or purchasing your first starter home, acquiring real estate in this corner of the Ottawa Valley is an exciting venture.

However, navigating the transaction smoothly requires looking beyond the agreed purchase price. Preparing for closing costs—the array of fees, adjustments, and taxes due on the day you pick up the keys—is paramount to avoiding last-minute financing stress. Below is a comprehensive breakdown curated by Prakash Solanki, REALTOR® at RE/MAX Pembroke Realty Ltd., Brokerage, to help you budget with confidence.

The General Rule of Thumb for Closing Costs

In Ontario, home buyers should generally reserve between 1.5% to 4% of the property’s total purchase price to cover closing costs. For a $450,000 family home in Laurentian Valley, this equates to roughly $6,750 to $18,000 above your initial down payment.

Step-by-Step Financial Roadmap: From Offer to Closing Day

1. The Good Faith Deposit & Down Payment

When your Agreement of Purchase and Sale is accepted in Laurentian Valley, you will typically need to submit a deposit (usually within 24 hours). This amount is held in trust by the listing brokerage (such as RE/MAX Pembroke Realty Ltd.) and counts directly toward your down payment upon closing. Down payments range from a minimum of 5% on homes up to $500,000, to 20% or more for conventional mortgages and non-standard rural properties.

2. Ontario Land Transfer Tax (LTT)

The provincial government levies a tax on all properties transferred in Ontario based on a sliding scale:

  • 0.5% on the first $55,000
  • 1.0% on amounts between $55,000 and $250,000
  • 1.5% on amounts between $250,000 and $400,000
  • 2.0% on amounts above $400,000 (up to $2,000,000)

First-Time Home Buyer Rebate: If you are buying your very first home in Ontario, you may qualify for a provincial Land Transfer Tax rebate of up to $4,000, which completely covers the LTT on properties valued up to $368,000.

3. Legal Fees and Disbursements

A licensed Ontario real estate lawyer must handle title registration, review contracts, conduct title searches, and organize the closing funds. Legal fees alongside disbursements (filing fees, software charges, and document couriers) typically average between $1,500 and $2,500 + HST.

4. Title Insurance

Almost all Canadian mortgage lenders require title insurance before funding a loan. Title insurance protects you and your lender against existing survey discrepancies, municipal work orders, zoning non-compliance, and title fraud. Depending on the size and scope of your property, expect to spend $350 to $800.

5. High-Ratio Mortgage Default Insurance (CMHC / Sagen)

If your down payment is less than 20%, you are required to purchase mortgage default insurance. While this premium is rolled directly into your mortgage principal balance, the 8% Ontario Provincial Sales Tax (PST) on the premium must be paid in cash on closing.

Laurentian Valley & Rural Renfrew County Specific Inspections

Many homes in Laurentian Valley enjoy acreage, country privacy, and scenic woods. However, non-municipal infrastructure requires specialized due diligence that every buyer should account for upfront:

Septic System Pumping & Inspection

For off-grid or rural homes, a professional inspection and tank pump-out typically costs between $400 and $800, ensuring the weeping bed and tank are free of structural failure.

Well Flow & Potability Testing

While public health units test bacteria for free, private flow-rate tests and comprehensive chemical analysis (potability) cost around $200 to $500.

WETT Inspection (Wood Burning Appliances)

If your prospective home features a wood stove, fireplace insert, or pellet stove, insurance companies often mandate a WETT inspection (approx. $150 to $300) prior to binding coverage.

Fuel Adjustments (Propane / Oil)

If the home uses oil or propane heating, the seller will top off the tank prior to closing. You must reimburse them on the Statement of Adjustments (typically $600 to $1,500).
